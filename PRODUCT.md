# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

DreamLog serves people who need a private, low-friction way to speak through their day, and therapists who want more useful session context without taking ownership of a client’s journal. Individual visitors are often arriving in a quiet, emotionally charged moment; clinicians are assessing whether the tool respects consent, boundaries, and their workflow.

## Product Purpose

DreamLog turns a spoken reflection into a private journal, a grounded response, and long-term emotional patterns. It helps people notice what recurs without presenting itself as therapy. Its therapist pathway enables a client to selectively share a time-limited summary for better sessions while retaining control of their underlying material.

## Brand Personality

Intimate, human, and steady. DreamLog should feel like a quiet place to return to: emotionally warm enough to invite honesty, precise enough to earn trust, and direct enough to prompt a first try.

## Anti-references

- Generic AI-wellness landing pages built from dark backgrounds, glowing orbs, floating cards, purple gradients, and feature-bento grids.
- Therapy products that imply clinical authority, make grand claims, or treat sensitive data as a product demo.
- Overly precious editorial pages that privilege aesthetic distance over a clear first action.
- One generic marketing journey serving users, therapists, and employers at once.

## Design Principles

1. Make the first step feel smaller than the thought it takes to begin.
2. Let privacy be visible as a user action, not a legal footnote.
3. Give individuals and therapists distinct, respectful paths from the first screen.
4. Use specific moments and real language instead of abstract AI promises.
5. Pair emotional warmth with clinical restraint; the product supports reflection, it does not replace care.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Meet WCAG 2.2 AA contrast and keyboard-navigation expectations. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`; motion must never conceal content or carry required meaning. Use clear language, support multilingual journaling including Hindi and Hinglish, and avoid colour-only status communication.
