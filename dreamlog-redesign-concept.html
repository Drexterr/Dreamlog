A visible privacy model

Nothing is shared by accident.

Privacy claims are easy to bury in a FAQ. This concept turns them into the product story for clinicians—and makes the client’s control legible before anyone registers.

Only the person journaling can select a summary for sharing. The therapist does not browse a client’s journal history.

A share link is generated by the client and protected by their chosen passcode, so the recipient is explicit rather than assumed.

The link has a short, stated lifespan. That keeps the sharing event bounded to the session it is meant to support.

Therapists see the shared mood trend and AI-generated brief—not a raw transcript or recording—matching the product’s stated client-control model.

Design rationale

What changes—and why.

Change: replace the current feature-led hero, 3D particle field, phone mockup, and floating cards with one transcript artifact and a two-path choice. Why: journaling is emotionally loaded; a recognisable line of thought is more persuasive than a crowded product demo.
Change: give individual journaling and therapist collaboration equal, distinct entry points; keep employer wellness on its own future page. Why: the current page has consumer pricing, corporate wellness, clinician copy, features, FAQ, and download CTA competing for one visitor’s attention.
Change: tell the therapist story through four user controls rather than generic “secure” language. Why: the codebase already supports client-created, passcode-protected, expiring links and shared summaries; that is concrete differentiation worth showing.
Change: reduce gold shimmer, ambient orbs, hover-lift cards, badges, repeated uppercase eyebrows, and fade-on-scroll choreography. Use solid fields, hairline rules, a transcript, offset paper, and direct copy. Why: those repeated SaaS conventions make a sensitive product feel generated rather than considered.
Change: lead with “one honest minute” and “begin before you feel ready,” not an abstract promise to understand yourself. Why: it offers a believable first action while preserving the warm, intimate tone you chose.
