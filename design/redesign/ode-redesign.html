Ode · voice journal · UI/UX redesign study

A quieter Ode, six ways

An audit of the current mobile app, the principles a redesign should keep, and six clickable directions. Each prototype covers the full loop: speak, wait, read the reflection, reply, browse past entries, see patterns, and find help. An independent design-critic agent scored all six over four rounds. Three now score above 80%.

1 · Where the app is today

The bones are calm. The surfaces around them are not.

Read from the code in mobile/app and src/theme.ts. Home is already disciplined; the further you go from Home, the louder it gets.

Keep

  • One primary action on HomeA single record button, with at most one contextual card above it (flashback or starter prompt, never both).
  • Empty states that stay emptyThe week strip hides until there is a recorded day, so new users meet no scaffolding.
  • Starter prompts without AI costDerived from morning_nudge and connection_insight, with a softer prompt after a 7-day gap.
  • The Breath Line mark and warm espresso paletteErode with Hanken Grotesk reads literary without feeling clinical.

Fix

  • Muted text is too faint to readtextMuted is 28% cream on espresso, about 2.3:1 contrast. WCAG body text needs 4.5:1. Date and tab labels are also 10px.
  • Screens get dense away from HomeSettings is 1,536 lines with about 39 tap targets; Mood is 1,036 lines; Reflection has about 23. The moment right after speaking should be the quietest one.
  • Navigation names don't match contentThe "Explore" tab opens the entry timeline. Therapy, Journeys, Chapters and People sit off-tab with no clear way in.
  • Hand-drawn tab iconsA ⌂ glyph, a dot grid, View-built bars and a ring. Weights and sizes don't match, and they're hard to recognise.
  • Eight goal palettesThe brand shifts per goal, and token names like purple400 now hold amber. That's eight palettes to QA for contrast.
  • The streak sits top-right on HomeA score in the first glance adds pressure, even with kind copy.
2 · What the redesign is for

An ode is spoken to someone. The app should feel like it's listening.

Every direction below follows these rules. They differ in metaphor and mood, not in the core flow.

Voice first, always one tapRecording is the default action on every Home and never sits behind a menu.
One thing per screenEach screen asks one question or shows one answer. Extra features move one level down.
Ode answers backThe reflection reads like a reply written to you, with up to 3 replies of your own.
Rhythm, not scorePatterns show how weeks feel. Streaks move out of the first glance.
Help is never hiddenCrisis resources are one tap from the profile screen in every design.
Readable in the darkSecondary text at 4.5:1 or better, 13px minimum, one brand palette per design.
