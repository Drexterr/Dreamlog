Ode · Identity, Round 2

The Breath Line

One continuous stroke that trembles like speech and settles into a single point of rest. It's the emotional arc of a journaling session — you speak, it steadies, you land — drawn in one gesture. This page refines the mark, then puts it everywhere it will actually live.

Wordmarks are shown in a stand-in serif; final artwork is set in Cormorant Garamond Light, the app's heading face. Palette is the app's own: espresso, amber, cream.

The tail

Ending the line without flatlining

Round 1 ended dead flat — and in a mental-health app, a trembling line that goes flat sits one association away from flatlining. Three revisions, each keeping the decay but changing where it lands. The dot version wins: the tremor resolves into a point of rest, and in the lockup that dot doubles as a full stop — ode, period. Said. Settled.

C · The settled dot

One last small breath, then a point of rest. Solves the flatline, gives the icon a focal anchor, and punctuates the wordmark like a full stop.

D · Softer waves

Rounder, slower tremor — furthest from an ECG trace. Keep this curvature in mind if C ever reads too clinical at final drawing.

All variants: single stroke, round caps, decaying amplitude left to right. The dot is 2× the stroke width.

Lockups

Three ways the mark and wordmark sit together

Primary lockup runs the line beneath the word, settling into the full stop just past it — the line literally underlines what was spoken. Stacked for square spaces, wordmark alone where the line has already appeared on screen.

Primary (horizontal) · stacked · light ground. On light grounds the amber deepens to #a06f35 for contrast.

App icon

Cropped, thickened, given a place to land

The full mark is too wide and too thin for an icon grid. The icon crops to the two most expressive waves plus the settle-and-dot, at a heavier stroke, so it fills the frame and holds its own next to filled icons. Espresso ground is the default; the amber-fill alternate exists if the dark icon ever disappears against a dark wallpaper.

Motion

The line is the loading state

Cold start: the line draws itself left to right — trembling, steadying — the dot lands, and ode fades up. About three seconds, which is what a cold start costs anyway; the logo absorbs the wait. The same stroke, looping, becomes the processing screen's "listening" state — so the brand gesture and the product's working state are one drawing.

Usage

Keeping the line honest

Clear space is x — the dot's diameter — on all sides. Below 20 px of height, drop the full mark and use the icon crop. The wordmark never appears without either the line or espresso-world styling around it.

40 px — full mark
24 px — minimum
below → icon crop

Don't flatten the tail — the dot is the resolution, not a decoration.

Don't sharpen the waves — spikes read as a heart monitor, not a voice.

Don't stretch or squash — the decay ratio is the drawing.

Don't recolor or gradient it — amber on espresso, deep amber on cream, or one-color silhouette.

Where this lands

The Breath Line works as the whole identity because it isn't just a logo — it's the product's one gesture, reused: the splash draws it, the processing screen loops it, the mood arc echoes it, the notification carries it. The settled dot fixes the flatline problem and gives every small rendering a focal point.
